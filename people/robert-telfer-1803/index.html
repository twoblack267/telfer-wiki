Family Stories

The Brother Who Stayed — A Life in Castleton

Of all the children of James Telfer and Betty Hutton, Robert was the one who lived and died within a few miles of where he was born. While his younger brother Francis crossed the world to South Australia, and his older brother James followed years later, Robert stayed put in the Liddesdale valley.

He married Elizabeth "Betty" Oliver around 1828 and they set up house in Castleton. The 1841 census shows them with four young children — Elizabeth, James, Jean, and John — living alongside an older John Telfer (64), Violet Telfer, and William Telfer. Extended family sharing a roof, as folks did in those days.

Robert worked as an agricultural labourer, the kind of life his father had lived as a hand loom weaver — hard work, modest means, family close. By 1851, things had changed. Robert was 46 and living with only two of his children still at home. His wife Betty was gone — whether she'd died or left, the records don't say.

He died in 1878 at age 74, in the same parish where he was born. The statutory death registration records his mother's maiden name as Hutton, confirming his place in the family.

Robert never saw Australia. Never sailed on a ship across the ocean. But his story matters too — the brother who stayed, kept the old name in the old country, and lived the quiet life that his more adventurous siblings left behind.

(From the Borders to the Bush, pp.17-20; 1841 and 1851 Scotland Censuses)

Life Summary

Robert Telfer was born in 1803 in Castleton, Roxburghshire, the fifth child and third son of James Telfer and Betty Hutton. He was baptised on 25 April 1803 in the Church of Scotland at Castleton parish, the same Liddesdale valley where the Telfer family had lived for generations.

Unlike his younger brother Francis, who emigrated to South Australia in 1838, Robert remained in the Scottish Borders his entire life. He married Elizabeth "Betty" Oliver around 1828. The 1841 census shows the couple living in Castleton with their young family, alongside Robert's older brother John Telfer (64), Violet Telfer (25), and William Telfer (22) — likely extended family sharing a household.

By 1851, Robert (now aged 46) was living in Castleton with only his daughters Elizabeth (17) and John (11) still at home. His wife Betty is absent from the 1851 census household — she may have died between 1841 and 1851, though her death has not been traced.

Robert's death at age 74 was registered in 1878 in the Castleton district. The statutory death registration records his mother's maiden surname as Hutton, confirming his parentage.
