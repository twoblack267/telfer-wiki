Gilbert Melville Telfer (the book records his given names as Gilbert Melville, not Melvin McLeod — he was known as "Mel") was born in 1911, the third child of Mark Telfer (1877–1946) and Julia Matilda Seebohm (1876–1939) of Tantanoola, South Australia.

He married Jessie Allan and they had three children — Beth, who married Clive Lipscombe; Ian, who married Sally Taylor; and Allan, who married Christine Marshall.

Mel was a good footballer, playing at centre wing and proving formidable as a fast player and a good ball handler. He was an opening batsman for the Tantanoola Cricket team and made many centuries. He was a most useful member of the team and excelled as the wicket keeper. When his eyesight no longer permitted him to play football and cricket, Mel played bowls, both indoor and outdoor, and proved to be an excellent player.

Like his father and brothers, he was interested in local affairs and was a Life Member of the Tantanoola Pastoral, Agricultural and Horticultural Society.

In his father's obituary, he was listed as "Mel Telfer, Tantanoola".

He married Jessie Allan and they had three children — Beth, Ian and Allan. (From the Borders to the Bush, p.243)
