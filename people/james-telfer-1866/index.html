Life Summary

James Telfer was the first child and eldest son of John Telfer (1840–1913) and his first wife Martha Allen (1840–1876). Born at Honeysuckle Flat, South Australia on 18 October 1866, James was the first of four children from this marriage and carried his paternal grandfather's name.

In 1895, James—then living at Tantanoola—was listed in the Border Watch Almanac as a newspaper agent for the district, suggesting he operated a local agency for the Mount Gambier newspaper. The 1894 Tantanoola Show concert featured him as a performer, and Border Watch advertisements from 1898 and 1900 confirm his newspaper agency at Tantanoola.

He married Alma Ida Sporer (1881–1965) on 24 October 1917 at the Lutheran Church, Mount Gambier — James was 49, Alma was 37. Their only child, Vida, was born on 8 March 1919. James became a prominent community figure in Glencoe West: he was appointed a Justice of the Peace in 1924, served as District Council Auditor (re-elected 1927), and acted as Secretary of the Glencoe District Hall in 1929.

He died on 28 August 1946 at Glencoe West, South Australia, aged 79. His obituary in The Advertiser (2 Sep 1946) and The Chronicle (5 Sep 1946) described him as the beloved husband of Alma and loving father of Vida.

Family Stories

James was the first of John and Martha Telfer's four children, born at Honeysuckle Flat on 18 October 1866. He moved with the family to Tantanoola as a young boy and quickly became involved in community life. He served as the first Secretary of the Tantanoola Agricultural and Floral Society and was also Secretary of the committee formed to organise the building of a Presbyterian Church in the town.

In 1917 he married Alma Ida Sporer at Mount Gambier, and together they established a general store at Glencoe West that also housed the local Post Office and sold groceries, hardware, drapery and anything else the farming community might need. James was an Elder at the local Presbyterian Church — a quiet, gentle soul who loved reading, especially poetry. He was Patron of the Glencoe West Tennis Club, served as its Auditor, and was an enthusiastic supporter of the Cricket Club. Always ready to lend a hand, James was a pillar of the community until his death in 1946. He and Alma had one daughter, Vida.

(From the Borders to the Bush, pp.235–236)
