Life Summary

Elizabeth "Betty" Hutton was born in 1774 and married James Telfer around 1795 in the Castleton / Liddesdale area of the Scottish Borders. She and James raised at least eight children at the family farm in Castleton parish, Roxburghshire.

Her maiden surname is confirmed by the Scottish Statutory Death Index registrations for her children — William Telfer's death entry records mother's maiden surname as "Hutton". This same surname appears in the WikiTree profile for her son Francis Telfer (Telfer-400), linking her as the mother of the Australian immigrant branch.

Betty outlived her husband by eight years. She died in April 1853 and was buried alongside James at Old Castleton Cemetery in Newcastleton.

Family Stories

The Woman at the Heart of the Clan

Elizabeth "Betty" Hutton was born in 1774 in the Scottish Borders and married James Telfer sometime around 1795. She would go on to be the mother of eight children — five sons and three daughters — and the matriarch of a family that spread from the Liddesdale valley to the other side of the world.

Life at Greenholm

Betty and James began their married life at Greenholm, a small farming area in Liddesdale near Newcastleton, Roxburghshire. There she gave birth to their first six children between 1796 and 1805: James, Isobel, Adam, Margaret, Robert, and John. Life for a hand-loom weaver's wife was demanding. The cottage was small, the winters were harsh, and there were always hungry mouths to feed. Betty managed her household, raised the children, and kept the family running while James worked the loom and tended the sheep.

The Move to Blackburnfoot

Sometime around 1805, the family moved a short distance north to a place called Blackburnfoot at the northern end of Newcastleton. The area had its own small weaving industry, which suited James's trade. It was there that Betty's last two children were born — Francis in February 1809, and William in April 1811. Blackburnfoot has since vanished — demolished when the railway line was built through Liddesdale in the 1840s — but it held the childhood home of the man who would one day cross the world.

The Widow's Years

James Telfer died in December 1845 at the age of 84, leaving Betty a widow after about 50 years of marriage. She outlived him by eight years, dying at Castleton on 14 April 1853 at the age of 79. She was laid to rest beside him in Old Castleton Cemetery. Their headstone reads simply: "Elizabeth Hutton his spouse" — a quiet marker for a woman who raised eight children through the hard Border winters, who watched most of them scatter, and whose son Francis would help build a new Telfer legacy in South Australia.

(From the Borders to the Bush, pp.17–20)
