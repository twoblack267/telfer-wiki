Life Summary

Francis Telfer was born on 2 February 1809 at Blackburn Foot (the old Black Burn fort) in Liddesdale, Roxburghshire, Scotland — the seventh of eight children born to James Telfer and Elizabeth "Betty" Hutton. He grew up in the Castleton region of the Scottish Borders.

The Voyage

Francis emigrated to South Australia aboard the Prince George, departing London in September 1838 and arriving at Port Adelaide on 26 December 1838. He travelled with his first wife Jean Murray and their three young children: James (6), Esther (4), and Elizabeth (2). A fourth child, John (the baby), died during the voyage.

Three Marriages

Francis outlived his first two wives. Jean Murray died shortly after arrival at the Emigration Depot in Adelaide on 22 January 1839 — barely a month after landing. Her infant son John had died during the voyage.

Within months, Francis married Dinah Flavel (a young English immigrant from Northamptonshire) on 4 May 1839. They had one daughter, Jean (born at Chain of Ponds, 1840). Dinah died on 20 March 1844 at Chain of Ponds, aged only 22, and was buried at West Terrace Cemetery, Adelaide.

On 27 March 1845, Francis married Margaret Wright, with whom he had three more children: John (the main line ancestor), Margaret Dougal, and George Wright. Margaret Wright brought stability to the household and outlived Francis, dying in 1892.

Later Life

Francis farmed at Chain of Ponds and Kersbrook in the Adelaide Hills before settling at Hope Farm, Tarlee, in the Clare Valley region. He became a respected local identity. By the time of his death at age 86, he had established the Telfer name across South Australia.

His obituary in The Border Watch (18 September 1895) recorded:
"He leaves two sons — Mr. John Telfer, of Hawker, and Mr. G. W. Telfer, Willaston — and three daughters — Mrs. Farrow, of Hope Farm, Mrs. Smith, of Canouba, New South Wales, and Mrs. A. F. Telfer, of Edgerston, Tantanoola."

Francis died at Hope Farm, Tarlee on 2 September 1895. A memorial headstone was erected by the Telfer Family History Association in 1999, commemorating his three wives and his place as the founder of the Australian Telfer line.

Family Stories

The Lost Boy — James Telfer (1832–1845)

Francis's eldest son James, aged 13, disappeared while bringing in stray sheep during heavy rain in July 1844 near Chain of Ponds. Neighbours, police — they scoured the flooded countryside for days. Nothing. The South Australian Register ran a notice on 10 Jul 1844 asking for information. James was never seen alive again.

Around 1877, floodwaters receded near Adelaide and a human skeleton was found lodged against a tree in the river. A doctor said it was a boy about 13, probably there 20+ years. Whether Francis ever found out is unknown.

It was the fourth family member Francis lost in six years: infant John (died at sea, 1838), wife Jean (1839), wife Dinah (1844), and his eldest son James.

(From the Borders to the Bush, pp.25–27; SA Register, 10 Jul 1844)

The Flavel Family and the Voyage of the Prince George

Francis sailed to South Australia on the Prince George alongside the Flavel family — Joseph and Mary Flavel and their seven kids. The Flavels became lifelong friends. William Flavel remembered arriving at Port Misery on 26 Dec 1838 and walking barefoot across the sandhills, where the summer sand was so hot he "had to run from one bush to another till I got clear of the sand." The Flavel baby, John Telfer, died during the voyage and was buried at sea.

The Prince George was a 482-ton, 118-foot, 3-masted barque under Captain Young. Francis applied for emigration on 9 August 1838, had just 26 days to prepare, and sailed from London on 4 September 1838.

(From the Borders to the Bush, pp.22–24)

Francis and the Kersbrook Church of Christ

Francis was an intensely religious man. He regularly walked 22 miles from Kersbrook to Adelaide for the "Scotch Kirk" (Gouger Street United Presbyterian Church). In 1863, he became a founding trustee of the Kersbrook Church of Christ Chapel and served as Sunday School Superintendent. He was described as "the pastor's right hand" alongside John McMillan.

(From the Borders to the Bush, pp.27–31)

Francis's Last Letter — To Jane Murray Perry

About a month before Francis died at age 86, he wrote a letter to his granddaughter Jane Murray Perry (the daughter of his daughter Jean/Jane Telfer). The letter was written at Hope Farm, Tarlee and spoke of his life, his belief in God, and his hopes for his family. It is one of the few surviving documents in Francis's own hand — a direct connection to the man who founded the Australian Telfer line.

(From the Borders to the Bush, p.33)

The House at Macoota

Francis's stone house at Section 6146, Kersbrook had walls 15 inches thick of local stone. Originally shingle-roofed (still preserved under later iron), it had a central passage, three bedrooms off the south side, a kitchen/dining room and drawing room with large fireplaces on the north, and a well about 50 yards from the back door. The land was so thick with wattle and banksia scrub you couldn't walk through it when first taken up.

(From the Borders to the Bush, pp.31–33)

Margaret Wright and the Stirling Castle Legend

Family legend had it that Francis's third wife Margaret Wright was "born in Stirling Castle" — though which bit of the castle was left for debate. Problem is, the last king to actually live there was James VI (1567–1625), long before Margaret was born. Colourful story, suspect geography.

(From the Borders to the Bush, p.27)
